San Francisco Contractor Rates: What to Expect in 2026

By Solid Bay Construction SF | Updated 2026

San Francisco construction labor costs consistently rank among the highest in the country — often 40–60% above the national average. If you're gathering quotes, it helps to know what's actually driving the number before you compare bids.

Typical Hourly Rates by Trade

TradeTypical SF Rate
General contractor / project supervision$120–$180/hr
Licensed electrician$130–$190/hr
Licensed plumber$130–$185/hr
Finish carpenter$95–$150/hr
Framing carpenter$85–$130/hr
Painter$65–$110/hr

Most full renovations aren't billed hourly at all — a design-build firm typically prices the whole project as a fixed contract, with these hourly figures baked into the total rather than itemized separately. Hourly rates matter most for smaller repair jobs and change orders.

Why San Francisco Costs More

  • Cost of living for skilled trades — SF's housing costs push wages up across every trade that wants to retain experienced crews
  • Permit and inspection overhead — DBI review, scheduling, and re-inspection cycles all add project time that has to be priced in
  • Access constraints — narrow lots, street parking permits for dumpsters and material delivery, and limited staging space slow every trade down
  • Older housing stock — unknowns behind the walls (knob-and-tube wiring, galvanized pipe, non-code framing) routinely turn a "simple" job into a more involved one

General Contractor Markup

Beyond labor and materials, a general contractor's fee typically runs 15–25% of total project cost, covering supervision, subcontractor coordination, permit management, and warranty. A design-build firm that keeps architects, engineers, and a permit expeditor in-house often prices closer to the lower end of that range, since it isn't paying separate markups to third-party consultants on top of its own.

How to Compare Bids Fairly

Two bids at very different totals usually aren't pricing the same scope. Before comparing numbers, confirm each bid includes the same finish level, the same permit and inspection responsibility, and the same payment schedule — a bid that looks 20% cheaper but excludes permits, disposal, or finish materials isn't actually cheaper.
