Whole-Home Renovation Cost Breakdown for a Cow Hollow Townhouse

By Solid Bay Construction SF | Updated 2026

Cow Hollow's narrow, multi-level townhouses — often with a garage-level entry and three or more stories above — carry their own renovation logistics and cost drivers. Here's a realistic breakdown by scope.

What Makes Cow Hollow Townhouses Distinct

Vertical, narrow floor plans

Most Cow Hollow townhouses stack living space over 3–4 narrow levels rather than spreading across one floor, which means renovation work — material staging, debris removal, trade sequencing — happens vertically through a stairwell rather than across an open single-story footprint. This adds labor time compared to a single-level home of the same square footage.

Garage-level entry considerations

Many of these townhouses have a ground-floor garage with living space above, which is exactly the "soft story" configuration SF's seismic ordinance targets — worth having assessed even if a renovation isn't primarily focused on structural work.

Shared walls with neighboring townhouses

Party walls shared with adjacent units mean acoustic performance and vibration during construction are bigger considerations than in a detached single-family home, and any structural work near a shared wall needs coordination with the adjacent owner in some cases.

Sequencing a Multi-Level Renovation

Because the levels stack vertically, we typically sequence a full Cow Hollow townhouse renovation top-down for demo and bottom-up for finishes, minimizing how many times materials and debris travel through the stairwell. Getting this sequencing right on a narrow, multi-story property has a real effect on both timeline and cost.
