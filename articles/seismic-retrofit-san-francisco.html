San Francisco sits near the San Andreas and Hayward faults, and the USGS puts strong odds on another major Bay Area earthquake in the coming decades. If your home was built before the mid-1990s, there's a good chance it doesn't meet current seismic standards.

Why It Matters in San Francisco

Two structural conditions show up again and again in San Francisco's older housing stock, and both are directly tied to earthquake performance.

Soft-story buildings

Many SF homes and multi-unit buildings have a ground floor with large openings — garage doors, big windows, or open commercial space — and little wall bracing to resist lateral movement. When the ground shakes, that open ground floor can rack sideways and collapse, even while the floors above stay largely intact. San Francisco's mandatory Soft Story Retrofit Program identified thousands of these buildings citywide.

Unbolted or inadequate foundations

Many pre-1960s SF homes were built with wood framing that simply sits on top of the foundation, or with cripple walls that aren't braced. During an earthquake, a house can literally slide off its foundation. Foundation bolting, cripple wall bracing, and shear wall installation address this directly.

Warning Signs

  • Home was built before 1980 (and especially before 1960)
  • Ground floor has a garage, large windows, or an open commercial storefront
  • Visible gaps or cracks between the house framing and foundation
  • Raised foundation with a crawl space and exposed cripple walls
  • You've never had a structural engineer or contractor inspect the foundation

If two or more of these apply, it's worth a professional assessment before you assume everything's fine.

What a Retrofit Involves

  1. Structural assessment — identifying cripple wall condition, foundation bolting status, and soft-story vulnerabilities.
  2. Foundation bolting — anchoring the wood framing of the house to the concrete foundation with structural bolts, preventing the house from sliding.
  3. Cripple wall bracing — adding plywood shear panels to short foundation walls so they resist lateral forces instead of racking and collapsing.
  4. Shear wall and steel moment frame installation for soft-story conditions, particularly in garage-level ground floors.
  5. Permitting through SF DBI — seismic work requires permits and inspection sign-off, both for safety and for resale/insurance purposes.
The real cost of skipping it: An un-retrofitted home can become a real liability at resale. Buyers, agents, and increasingly lenders and insurers factor seismic compliance into their decisions. A retrofit isn't just a safety project — it's a value-preservation project.

FAQ

How much does seismic retrofitting cost in San Francisco?

Cost varies widely based on foundation type, square footage, and whether soft-story work is involved. A proper on-site assessment is the only way to get an accurate number — general online estimates rarely reflect SF-specific conditions.

Is seismic retrofit work required by law in San Francisco?

Certain soft-story buildings are subject to San Francisco's mandatory retrofit ordinance. Single-family homes aren't currently mandated, but insurance, resale, and safety considerations make it strongly recommended for older housing stock.

Do I need a permit for foundation bolting?

Yes. Structural seismic work requires a permit from San Francisco's Department of Building Inspection and final inspection sign-off.
