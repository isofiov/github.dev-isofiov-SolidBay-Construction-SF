Restoring Leaded & Stained Glass Windows in San Francisco Victorians

By Solid Bay Construction SF | Updated 2026

Original leaded and stained glass is one of the most distinctive, and most fragile, details in San Francisco's Victorian and Edwardian housing stock. Restoring it correctly is a specialized craft — very different from a standard window replacement project.

Common Problems in Century-Old Leaded Glass

  • Bowing or sagging: The lead came (the metal strips holding glass pieces) stretches over 100+ years, causing the panel to bulge or sag under its own weight
  • Broken or cracked came: Lead fatigue leads to cracking at solder joints, which can eventually let panels fall apart
  • Missing or replaced glass: Older repairs sometimes used mismatched glass, which is noticeable and reduces both authenticity and value
  • Failed exterior protective glazing: Many panels have a protective storm-glass layer outside them that's failed or fogged, exposing the leaded glass to weather

Repair vs. Full Releading

Minor cracks and a handful of broken solder joints can often be repaired in place. A panel that's visibly bowing, has multiple broken came sections, or has been improperly repaired before usually needs full releading — the panel is carefully disassembled, the original glass is cleaned and reused wherever possible, and new came is fitted and soldered following the original pattern.

What to Look for in a Restoration Specialist

  • Portfolio of prior leaded/stained glass work, specifically — this is a different trade than general window replacement
  • Willingness to reuse original glass rather than defaulting to full replacement, which preserves both character and value
  • A plan for protecting the panel during any surrounding construction work (a releaded panel is fragile until reinstalled)
