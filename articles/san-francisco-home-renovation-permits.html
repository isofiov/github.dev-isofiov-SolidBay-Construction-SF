Navigating San Francisco's building permit process is one of the most challenging aspects of any renovation project in the city. SF has one of the most complex permitting systems in California — with multiple departments, neighborhood reviews, and historic preservation requirements that can add months to your timeline.

Why SF Permits Are Different

San Francisco's permitting involves up to four city departments: the Department of Building Inspection (DBI), the Planning Department, the Fire Department, and the Department of Public Works. Most projects in other California cities only require DBI. In SF, understanding which departments are involved — and in what order — is critical to your timeline.

What Requires a Permit in San Francisco?

  • Any structural work — walls, beams, foundations
  • Electrical work beyond replacing fixtures in the same location
  • Plumbing changes or relocations
  • HVAC installation or major modifications
  • Additions and ADUs
  • Exterior changes in many neighborhoods
  • Window replacements in historic districts

What Doesn't Need a Permit: Painting, flooring replacement, cabinet replacement in same locations, appliance replacement, and minor repairs typically don't require permits in SF. But always confirm with your contractor — the rules are complex and change frequently.

The SF Permit Process Step by Step

Step 1: Pre-Application Meeting

For complex projects, a pre-application meeting with Planning or DBI can save weeks of wasted effort. Our team attends these meetings regularly and can advise on the fastest path to approval.

Step 2: Plan Preparation

Licensed architects and engineers prepare construction documents that meet SF's specific code requirements. Poor drawings are the #1 cause of permit delays — incomplete or incorrect plans get sent back for corrections, adding weeks to your timeline.

Step 3: Application Submission

Applications are submitted to DBI and Planning if required. SF now accepts electronic submissions for most projects, which has improved processing times significantly.

Step 4: Plan Check

DBI reviews drawings for code compliance. First-time submissions often receive correction letters requiring plan revisions. Experienced contractors minimize corrections by submitting complete, accurate drawings the first time.

Step 5: Inspections

DBI inspectors visit during construction to verify work matches approved plans. Required inspections vary — framing, rough electrical, rough plumbing, insulation, drywall, and final inspection are common for major renovations.

How to Speed Up SF Permits

  • Use an experienced permit expeditor with established DBI relationships
  • Submit complete, accurate drawings the first time
  • Respond to correction letters within 24 hours
  • Use SF's Pre-Approved ADU plans where applicable
  • Request over-the-counter review for qualifying projects

Historic Districts and Design Review

Much of San Francisco falls within historic districts where exterior changes require additional Planning Department review. This includes most of the Mission, Castro, Haight, Pacific Heights, and many other neighborhoods. Exterior changes — new windows, façade modifications, paint colors — require Planning approval even for seemingly minor work.
