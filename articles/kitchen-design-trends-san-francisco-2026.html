Top 7 Kitchen Design Trends for San Francisco Homes in 2026

By Solid Bay Construction SF | Updated 2026

SF kitchens have a specific set of constraints: narrow Victorian and Edwardian footprints, load-bearing walls between the kitchen and dining room, and a city that's pushing hard toward all-electric homes. Here's what we're actually specifying on current kitchen remodels — not a generic national trend list.

1. Warm wood tones replacing all-white cabinetry

The all-white kitchen that dominated the last decade is giving way to warmer palettes — white oak, walnut, and rift-cut oak cabinetry, often paired with a contrasting island in a deeper tone. It reads warmer in SF's frequently overcast light than stark white does.

2. Waterfall quartzite and marble islands

A waterfall edge — where the countertop material wraps down the sides of the island to the floor — remains one of the highest-impact single design choices in a kitchen remodel. Quartzite is edging out marble for clients who want the look with less maintenance.

3. Hidden scullery pantries for small SF kitchens

Many SF homes don't have room for a large, showpiece kitchen. Scullery pantries — a small working kitchen tucked behind the main one, hidden behind a door or pocket panel — let the visible kitchen stay clean and photogenic while dishes, small appliances, and prep mess stay out of sight. It's the single best space-maximizing trend we're seeing in SF's compact floor plans.

4. Panel-ready, integrated appliances

Refrigerators, dishwashers, and sometimes even ranges are increasingly clad in matching cabinetry panels rather than showing stainless steel fronts. It's a cleaner look that reads as custom millwork rather than "kitchen with appliances in it."

5. Induction cooktops replacing gas

Beyond personal preference, California and San Francisco have been steadily tightening gas appliance rules in new construction and major renovations. Induction has closed the performance gap with gas significantly, and we're specifying it on the large majority of full kitchen remodels now.

6. Mixed metal hardware — matte black and unlacquered brass

Single-finish hardware is out. Matte black plumbing fixtures paired with unlacquered brass cabinet hardware (which develops a living patina over time) is the combination we're getting asked for most often.

7. Statement lighting over the island

A single oversized fixture or a cluster of sculptural pendants over the island has become a default expectation rather than an upgrade — often the first thing a homeowner points to in inspiration photos.
