Custom Home vs. Full Remodel: When Rebuilding Makes More Sense

By Solid Bay Construction SF | Updated 2026

At a certain scope of work, a full remodel and a teardown rebuild start costing similar amounts — and once that happens, the decision becomes about more than just price. Here's how to think through it.

When Remodeling Still Wins

  • The existing foundation and structural frame are sound and don't need major work
  • You want to preserve historic character (Victorian/Edwardian details, original millwork) that would be lost in a rebuild
  • Your lot's existing setbacks or non-conforming status would be lost if you rebuilt from scratch — many older SF homes have legal non-conforming setbacks that a new build would have to meet current zoning for
  • You're not changing the footprint or roofline significantly

When Rebuilding Starts Making Sense

  • The remodel scope already requires touching the foundation, most structural framing, and most systems — at that point you're paying remodel-level costs without new-construction-level results
  • You want a significantly different floor plan than the existing structure can efficiently support
  • The home has significant unresolved issues (foundation failure, extensive dry rot, code violations) that make "renovate around it" more expensive than starting clean
  • You want current energy performance (insulation, windows, systems) throughout, not just in renovated areas

The Real Cost Comparison

ApproachTypical Cost (SF)
Full remodel, keeping structure/foundation$400–$650/sq ft
Full remodel requiring foundation & major structural work$550–$800/sq ft
Teardown + new custom home$650–$1,000+/sq ft

Once a remodel's scope pushes into the $550-800/sq ft range because of foundation and structural work, the cost gap to a full rebuild often narrows to 15-25% — at which point the decision becomes about what you actually get for that difference, not just the sticker price.

Non-Cost Factors That Matter

A teardown means starting the permit process over as new construction, which can mean a longer timeline in some SF neighborhoods with design review, but it also means the entire home is built to current code and energy standards, with no legacy systems or hidden conditions. A remodel typically keeps your existing lot coverage and setback rights, which can matter significantly in denser SF neighborhoods where current zoning wouldn't allow rebuilding at the same footprint.
