Basement Waterproofing Before an ADU or Living-Space Conversion

By Solid Bay Construction SF | Updated 2026

A basement that's been "fine" as storage space for decades can fail fast once it's finished as a living space or ADU — carpet, drywall, and furniture are far less forgiving of moisture than bare concrete and cardboard boxes. Waterproofing has to happen before finishing, not after.

Why San Francisco Basements Are Prone to Moisture

  • Much of the city's older housing stock has minimal or no foundation drainage installed when originally built
  • Grading around many SF homes, especially on sloped lots, directs water toward rather than away from the foundation
  • Older foundations often lack a proper vapor barrier, allowing moisture to migrate through the concrete itself even without visible leaks

Diagnosing the Source Before Treating It

Not all basement moisture comes from the same place, and treating the wrong source wastes money:

  • Surface/grading issues: Water pooling against the foundation from poor exterior grading or missing gutters/downspout extensions
  • Hydrostatic pressure: Groundwater pushing against the foundation from below or the side, common on sites with a high water table or poor soil drainage
  • Condensation: Humid air meeting a cool concrete surface, which looks like a leak but is actually a ventilation/humidity issue

What Waterproofing Actually Involves

ApproachWhen It's Used
Exterior grading & drainage correctionFirst step for surface-water issues — often the most cost-effective fix
Interior French drain + sump pumpManages hydrostatic pressure by collecting and pumping water out before it reaches the living space
Exterior foundation waterproofing membraneMost effective but most invasive — requires excavating around the foundation, typically done alongside other foundation work
Vapor barrier + dehumidificationAddresses condensation-driven moisture rather than active water intrusion

Why This Has to Happen Before ADU Conversion

California's building code requires habitable space to meet moisture-control standards, and DBI inspection for a basement ADU conversion will flag unresolved moisture issues. Beyond passing inspection, finishing a basement without addressing the actual water source just relocates the problem — mold behind new drywall, warped flooring, and a failed inspection are all far more expensive to fix after the fact than the waterproofing itself would have cost upfront.
