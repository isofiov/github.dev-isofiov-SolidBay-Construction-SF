San Francisco sits atop one of the most seismically active regions in North America. The Hayward Fault — capable of producing a 7.0+ magnitude earthquake — is widely considered overdue for a major rupture. For SF homeowners, seismic retrofitting isn't optional: it's a fundamental protection for your family and your investment.

What is a Seismic Retrofit?

A seismic retrofit strengthens the connection between your home's wooden frame and its concrete foundation — the critical weak point in most pre-1980 SF homes. During an earthquake, unretrofitted homes can slide off their foundations or have their cripple walls collapse, causing catastrophic structural damage.

The most common retrofit type in SF is the cripple wall retrofit: adding plywood sheathing to the short wood-framed walls between the foundation and first floor, and bolting the mudsill to the concrete foundation with anchor bolts.

Does Your SF Home Need Retrofitting?

Your home likely needs retrofitting if it was built before 1980, has a raised wood floor (not a concrete slab), has a soft story such as a garage at the ground floor, has not been previously retrofitted, or is a wood-frame building with a cripple wall.

SF Mandatory Retrofit Program: San Francisco's mandatory soft-story retrofit program (Ordinance 66-13) requires all wood-frame buildings with 5+ units and soft-story conditions to be retrofitted. Single-family homes are not currently required but are strongly recommended given SF's seismic risk profile.

Types of Seismic Retrofits in San Francisco

Cripple Wall Retrofit (Most Common)

Adds plywood sheathing to cripple walls and anchor bolts to the mudsill. Appropriate for most single-family and small multi-family homes. Cost: $10,000–$20,000. Can often be permitted over-the-counter with pre-approved plans.

Soft-Story Retrofit

Required for buildings with open ground floors such as garages or commercial spaces below residential. Adds steel moment frames or shear walls to the weak ground story. Cost: $50,000–$200,000+ depending on building size and configuration.

Full Foundation Upgrade

For homes with inadequate foundations, a complete foundation replacement may be required. Cost: $80,000–$250,000. Usually combined with other major renovation work.

Benefits of Seismic Retrofitting

  • Dramatically reduces risk of home sliding off foundation during major earthquake
  • Can reduce earthquake insurance premiums by 15–25%
  • Increases home value — retrofitted homes sell better in SF
  • Required for some refinancing and home equity loan programs
  • Peace of mind for your family in one of the world's most seismically active cities

Combining Retrofit with Renovation

The most cost-effective approach is to bundle seismic retrofitting with other renovation work. When you're already opening walls for a kitchen remodel or bathroom renovation, adding seismic work shares permit costs and contractor mobilization. SolidBay routinely bundles seismic retrofitting with larger renovation projects — saving homeowners 20–30% compared to doing the retrofit as a standalone project.

California Earthquake Brace and Bolt Program

California's Earthquake Brace and Bolt (EBB) program provides grants of up to $3,000 to help qualifying homeowners retrofit their homes. Check earthquakebracebolt.com for current program availability and eligibility requirements.
