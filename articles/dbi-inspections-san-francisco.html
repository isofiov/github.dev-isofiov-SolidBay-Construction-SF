SF DBI Inspections: What Happens During a Building Inspection

By Solid Bay Construction SF | Updated 2026

Your permit is approved and construction is underway — now DBI inspectors verify the work matches approved plans and current code, at specific points throughout the project. Here's what actually happens at each visit.

The Typical Inspection Sequence

  1. Foundation / rebar inspection — before concrete is poured, verifying rebar placement and forms match approved structural plans
  2. Rough framing inspection — after framing but before drywall, checking structural connections, hold-downs, and dimensions against plans
  3. Rough electrical, plumbing, and mechanical — after wiring, pipes, and ductwork are run but before they're covered, verifying code compliance (often scheduled as separate visits)
  4. Insulation inspection — confirming insulation type and R-value meets Title 24 energy code before drywall closes the walls
  5. Final inspection — after all work is complete, verifying the finished project matches approved plans and all prior corrections were made

How to Prepare for an Inspection

  • Have the approved plan set on site and accessible to the inspector
  • Make sure the specific area being inspected is actually accessible — not blocked by materials or other trades' work
  • Have your contractor or a knowledgeable representative present to answer questions
  • Address any items noted at a prior inspection before scheduling the next one

What Happens If You Fail an Inspection

Failing an inspection isn't unusual and isn't a permit denial — the inspector documents specific corrections needed, and you schedule a re-inspection once they're addressed. The real cost is time: each re-inspection cycle typically adds several days to a week to the schedule, which is why experienced contractors prepare thoroughly rather than treating inspections as a formality.

Same-Day vs. Scheduled Inspections

SF DBI inspections are typically requested online or by phone the day before, with a general morning or afternoon arrival window rather than an exact time — plan for some flexibility in your schedule on inspection days.
