Asbestos in San Francisco Homes: Testing, Abatement & Cost

By Solid Bay Construction SF | Updated 2026

Most of San Francisco's housing was built before 1980, when asbestos was routinely used in insulation, floor tile, popcorn ceilings, and duct wrap. Finding it isn't unusual — and it isn't a crisis, as long as it's identified and handled correctly before demolition starts.

Where Asbestos Typically Shows Up

  • Vermiculite attic insulation, common in homes built through the 1970s
  • Vinyl floor tile and the black mastic adhesive underneath it
  • Textured "popcorn" ceilings applied before the mid-1980s
  • Duct wrap and pipe insulation around older HVAC and plumbing runs
  • Some stucco and plaster mixes from the same era

When Testing Is Required

The Bay Area Air Quality Management District (BAAQMD) requires an asbestos survey before most demolition or renovation permits are issued for buildings constructed before 1980 — this is standard practice on nearly every older SF remodel, not an exception. Your contractor or permit expeditor typically arranges the survey as part of pre-construction.

Abatement Costs

ScopeTypical Cost
Testing / survey$400–$900
Localized removal (floor tile, small area)$2,000–$6,000
Whole-house abatement (attic, multiple rooms)$8,000–$25,000+

Cost depends on the material type, how much needs to be removed, and whether it can be safely encapsulated rather than removed entirely — encapsulation is sometimes an option for materials in good condition that won't be disturbed.

How It Fits Into Your Timeline

Abatement happens before demolition, performed by a licensed abatement contractor (a separate license from general contracting) working under BAAQMD notification requirements. A straightforward abatement adds roughly 1–3 weeks to a project schedule — longer if the survey reveals more extensive material than expected. Building this into your timeline upfront avoids a surprise delay once demo starts.
