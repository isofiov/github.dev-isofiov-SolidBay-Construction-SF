Kitchen Remodel Cost Guide for San Francisco Victorians & Edwardians

By Solid Bay Construction SF | Updated 2026

San Francisco's Victorian and Edwardian homes — most built between 1880 and 1915 — almost never have the kitchen a homeowner actually wants today. Here's why remodeling one costs more than the citywide average, and what to budget for.

Why These Kitchens Cost More to Remodel

The original layout works against you

Victorian and Edwardian kitchens were typically tucked into a narrow room at the rear of the house, separated from the dining room by a load-bearing wall. Opening that layout into the kitchen most homeowners want today means structural engineering, a new beam, and permitting — not just new cabinets.

Hidden systems need replacing anyway

Once walls are open, knob-and-tube wiring, galvanized plumbing, and undersized electrical panels almost always need to come out. Budgeting for this as part of the kitchen project — rather than discovering it mid-construction — keeps the timeline and budget predictable.

Matching period details takes more labor

Where a kitchen remodel touches adjacent original trim, wainscoting, or door casings, matching that period detailing by hand costs more than the paint-grade trim used in a standard remodel.

What Drives the Premium

  • Structural engineering and permitting for wall removal
  • Electrical and plumbing system replacement
  • Custom millwork to match existing trim
  • Uneven original framing requiring more prep before new cabinetry installs cleanly

Getting an Accurate Number for Your Home

Because so much of the cost depends on hidden conditions behind existing walls, an accurate estimate for a Victorian or Edwardian kitchen requires an in-person site visit — not a square-footage formula. We identify what's structural, what's original systems, and what's cosmetic before pricing anything.
