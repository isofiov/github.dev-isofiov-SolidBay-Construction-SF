HOW TO CHOOSE THE BEST GENERAL CONTRACTOR IN SAN FRANCISCO

Complete guide to choosing the best general contractor in San Francisco. The exact questions to ask, red flags to avoid, and process to follow before signing a contract.

Choosing the right general contractor is the single most important decision in any San Francisco renovation project. The wrong choice can mean cost overruns, legal disputes, failed inspections, and workmanship that has to be torn out and redone. The right choice means a project delivered on time, on budget, and with quality that lasts decades.

Step 1: Verify the License

Every contractor doing more than $500 in work in California must be licensed by the Contractors State License Board (CSLB). Verify any contractor's license at cslb.ca.gov before going further. Check that the license is active, the license type matches the work (Class B for general construction), and there are no disciplinary actions on record.

Red Flag: Any contractor who can't immediately provide their CSLB license number, or whose license is expired or has disciplinary actions, should be disqualified immediately — regardless of how compelling their pitch is.

Step 2: Verify Insurance

Require certificates of insurance for general liability (minimum $1,000,000 per occurrence), workers' compensation (required if they have any employees), and auto liability. Request that you be named as an additional insured on their general liability policy — this protects you if someone is injured on your property during the project.

Step 3: Get SF-Specific References

General construction experience is not the same as SF experience. The city's permitting process, historic preservation requirements, seismic codes, and building stock are unique. Ask for three references from SF projects completed in the last two years — and actually call them.

When you call references, ask: Did they finish on time? On budget? Would you hire them again without hesitation?

Step 4: Get Itemized Bids

Get at least three bids for any significant project. Require that bids be fully itemized — line by line by trade and material. A bid that just says "Kitchen remodel: $120,000" tells you nothing. You need to see demolition, framing, plumbing rough and finish, electrical rough and finish, tile, cabinetry, permits, and contingency broken out separately.

Step 5: Understand the Contract

California law requires written contracts for any project over $500. Your contract should include detailed scope of work, payment schedule tied to milestones (not dates), change order process and pricing, start and completion dates, permit responsibility, and dispute resolution process.

Questions to Ask Every Contractor

  • Who will be my primary point of contact throughout the project?
  • Will you be on-site daily, or will you subcontract management?
  • Which subcontractors will you use, and have you worked with them before?
  • How do you handle unexpected conditions discovered during construction?
  • Who handles permit applications and inspections?
  • What is your change order process?
