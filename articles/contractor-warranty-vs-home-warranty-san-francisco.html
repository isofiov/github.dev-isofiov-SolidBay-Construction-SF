Contractor Warranty vs. Home Warranty: What's Actually Covered

By Solid Bay Construction SF | Updated 2026

"Warranty" gets used loosely in home renovation, and it covers at least three genuinely different things: your contractor's workmanship warranty, a home warranty service plan, and manufacturer warranties on the fixtures and appliances themselves. Knowing which one applies before something goes wrong saves a lot of frustration after the job is done.

What a Contractor's Workmanship Warranty Covers

This is the contractor's promise that their labor and installation was done correctly. If a tile installation cracks because it wasn't set properly, if a door doesn't hang right, or if a newly installed fixture leaks because of an installation error, that's a workmanship warranty claim against your contractor — not a home warranty issue. Get the specific warranty period and scope in writing as part of your contract, not as a verbal promise. Typical terms run 1-2 years for general workmanship, though some contractors warranty structural work longer.

What a Home Warranty Covers (and Doesn't)

A home warranty is a separate service contract, usually purchased annually, that covers the repair or replacement of home systems and appliances (HVAC, water heater, plumbing, electrical) when they fail from normal wear and age — not from installation defects. Critically, most home warranty companies will deny a claim if they determine the failure traces back to improper installation during a recent remodel, which routes you back to the contractor's workmanship warranty instead.

Manufacturer Warranties on Fixtures and Appliances

Separately, the individual products installed during your remodel — the range, the water heater, the plumbing fixtures — carry their own manufacturer warranties, typically covering defects in the product itself for anywhere from 1 to 10+ years depending on the item. Keep your product manuals and registration confirmations; many manufacturers require product registration within a set window after installation for the warranty to apply.

What Happens If Something Goes Wrong After the Job Is Done

  1. Identify what actually failed — a product defect, an installation error, or normal wear
  2. If it's an installation issue, contact your contractor directly under their workmanship warranty first
  3. If it's a product defect, the manufacturer's warranty applies, though your contractor can often help facilitate the claim since they have the install documentation
  4. Document everything — photos, dates, and any correspondence — from the moment you notice the issue

A contractor who stands behind their work will still be reachable and responsive well after final payment. That responsiveness — more than the specific number of months on paper — is usually the real difference between a warranty that means something and one that doesn't.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a contractor's workmanship warranty typically last?

1-2 years is common for general workmanship, with structural work sometimes warrantied longer. Always get the specific period and scope in writing as part of your contract.

Does a home warranty cover a leak caused by a remodel?

Usually not, if the leak stems from installation error — that falls under the contractor's workmanship warranty rather than a home warranty, which covers normal wear rather than construction defects.
