Junior ADU vs. Full ADU in San Francisco: Which Should You Build?

By Solid Bay Construction SF | Updated 2026

Both a Junior ADU and a full ADU let you add housing on your existing lot — but they're built differently, cost differently, and serve different goals. Here's how to tell which one fits what you're trying to do.

Side-by-Side Comparison

Junior ADU (JADU)Full ADU
Max size500 sq ftNo fixed cap; typically 800–1,200 sq ft
KitchenKitchenette (efficiency kitchen) onlyFull kitchen required
BathroomCan share with main house, or have its ownMust have its own
LocationMust be within the existing structure of the homeCan be within, attached, or fully detached

Best forLower-cost path to a rentable or family space within your existing footprintMaximum rental income or a fully independent living situation

When a JADU Makes More Sense

A JADU is the lower-cost, lower-disruption option because it's built within your home's existing walls — converting an existing room or basement area rather than adding new structure. It's a strong fit if you want a modest income unit or space for a family member without the cost of a full separate unit, and you're comfortable sharing a bathroom or building a small one within the existing footprint.

When a Full ADU Makes More Sense

If your goal is maximum rental income, a fully independent unit for long-term tenants, or a garage conversion that becomes a genuine separate home, a full ADU is the right category. It costs more, but a complete kitchen, private bathroom, and separate entrance make it far more marketable as a long-term rental.

Can You Build Both?

Yes — under current California law, most San Francisco single-family properties can build one ADU and one JADU on the same lot, both by right without a discretionary hearing. This is an increasingly common approach for homeowners who want both a rental unit and a smaller space for family, built in a single permitting and construction process.
