Do You Need a Structural Engineer for Your San Francisco Renovation?

By Solid Bay Construction SF | Updated 2026

Not every renovation needs a structural engineer — a kitchen refresh or cosmetic bathroom update usually doesn't touch the structure at all. But a specific set of common SF projects legally require one, and skipping it isn't a shortcut worth taking.

Projects That Require a Structural Engineer

  • Load-bearing wall removal — required to size the replacement beam and confirm the load path
  • Home additions — 2nd story and horizontal additions both require confirming the existing structure can support the new load
  • Foundation work and retrofits — new foundation walls, piers, and seismic bracing all require engineered calculations
  • ADU construction — both new construction and conversions typically require structural review, especially garage conversions where the existing structure wasn't built for habitable space
  • Seismic retrofit — cripple wall bracing and foundation bolting require engineering to meet current code

What a Structural Engineer Actually Does

A structural engineer reviews the existing framing and foundation, determines the load path through the building, and produces stamped calculations and drawings specifying exactly how new structural elements — beams, posts, foundation reinforcement — need to be sized and installed. These stamped drawings are what SF DBI requires before issuing a permit for structural work, and what the DBI framing inspector checks the completed work against.

What It Costs

Structural engineering fees scale with project complexity — a single wall removal is a relatively small fee, while a full addition or foundation redesign involves considerably more engineering work. On a design-build project, this cost is typically rolled into the overall project estimate rather than billed as a separate, surprise line item.

Why In-House Structural Engineering Matters

When the structural engineer, architect, and construction team are separate companies, changes discovered mid-project — an unexpected condition behind a wall, a beam that doesn't fit as drawn — require a round-trip between multiple firms to resolve. With a structural engineer on the same team as the designers and builders, that coordination happens directly and quickly, which is one of the practical advantages of a design-build model on any project involving real structural work.
