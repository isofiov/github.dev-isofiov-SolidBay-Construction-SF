Load-Bearing Wall Removal in San Francisco: Cost, Process & Permits

By Solid Bay Construction SF | Updated 2026

SF's Victorian and Edwardian homes were built with compartmentalized rooms, and opening up a floor plan almost always means removing a wall that's carrying structural load. It's one of the highest-impact renovation moves available — and one where doing it without proper engineering can genuinely compromise the building.

Typical Cost

Removing a load-bearing wall with a new beam in San Francisco typically costs $25,000–$60,000, covering:

  • Structural engineering and stamped calculations
  • DBI permit submission
  • The new beam (steel or engineered lumber) and supporting posts
  • Temporary shoring during construction
  • Drywall, flooring, and finish repair around the new opening

Cost varies with the span being opened, whether the beam needs to be steel (typically required for wider spans) versus engineered lumber, and whether the wall carries load from more than one floor above.

How to Actually Tell If a Wall Is Load-Bearing

You can't reliably determine this by eye. A structural engineer reviews the framing direction of the floor and roof joists above the wall, whether the wall is stacked directly above another wall on a lower floor, and the overall load path of the structure. Common indicators — exterior walls, walls perpendicular to joists, walls in the center of the home — are useful clues, not proof. Assuming a wall is non-load-bearing and removing it without engineering is exactly how homes end up with sagging floors and cracked finishes.

The Process

  1. Structural assessment — an engineer determines the load path and specifies the replacement beam size
  2. Permit submission — stamped structural drawings go to SF DBI for plan check
  3. Temporary shoring — the load is temporarily supported before the wall comes out
  4. Beam installation — the new beam and posts are set to carry the load permanently
  5. Framing inspection — DBI inspects the structural work before it's closed up
  6. Finish work — drywall, flooring, and paint restore the space

Why This Is a Job for an In-House Structural Engineer, Not a Guess

Because load-bearing wall removal directly affects the structural integrity of the building, it's one of the clearest examples of why a design-build contractor with in-house structural engineering matters — the engineering, permitting, and construction stay coordinated under one team instead of being handed off between separate parties.
