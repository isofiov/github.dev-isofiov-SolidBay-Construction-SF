WHY SEISMIC RETROFIT YOUR MARIN COUNTY HOME?

Most pre-1980 homes in Marin County have cripple walls — short wood-framed walls between the foundation and the first floor — that are highly vulnerable to earthquake damage. During a major earthquake, these walls can collapse, causing the home to slide off its foundation. A proper seismic retrofit installs plywood sheathing on cripple walls and anchor bolts connecting the structure to the foundation — dramatically improving the home's ability to withstand earthquake forces. In Marin County, the most vulnerable homes include hillside properties on stilts or posts, older Craftsman and Victorian homes with unreinforced cripple walls, and soft-story structures. SolidBay's in-house structural engineers assess each home's specific vulnerabilities and design an appropriate retrofit solution.

Seismic retrofit costs in Marin County typically range from $8,000–$30,000 for standard cripple wall retrofits, and $30,000–$100,000+ for more complex hillside or soft-story retrofits. The California Earthquake Brace and Bolt (EBB) program offers grants of up to $3,000 for eligible homeowners to retrofit wood-frame houses. Our team handles the entire process — structural assessment, permit drawings, permit submission to your Marin city's building department, and complete construction. All SolidBay seismic retrofits are engineered by our in-house structural engineers and comply with the latest FEMA P-1100 guidelines.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

How much does seismic retrofitting cost in Marin County?
Standard cripple wall retrofits typically cost $8,000–$30,000. Complex hillside or soft-story retrofits run $30,000–$100,000+. The EBB program offers grants up to $3,000 for eligible homes.
How long does a seismic retrofit take in Marin County?
Most standard seismic retrofits take 1–3 weeks for construction once permits are approved. The permit process typically takes 2–6 weeks depending on the city.
Does my Marin County home need a seismic retrofit?
Most pre-1980 homes with cripple walls benefit from retrofitting. Hillside and post-and-pier homes are particularly vulnerable. Our structural engineers can assess your specific situation.
Is seismic retrofitting required in Marin County?
Some Marin cities have mandatory retrofit programs for certain building types. Even where not required, retrofitting is strongly recommended given Marin County's seismic risk.
