2nd Story Addition vs. Rear Addition vs. ADU: Which Is Right for Your SF Home?

By Solid Bay Construction SF | Updated 2026

Almost every SF homeowner who needs more space ends up choosing between the same three options: build up, build back, or build a separate unit. Each solves a different problem — here's how to tell which one is actually yours.

The Three Options at a Glance

Addition TypeTypical CostBest For
2nd story / pop-top addition$400–$700/sq ftSmall lots with no room to build out
Rear / horizontal addition$400–$700/sq ftHomes with usable rear yard space
ADU (garage, basement, or detached)$180,000–$550,000+Rental income, in-laws, or a fully separate unit

2nd Story Addition: When Your Lot Won't Let You Go Back

Many SF lots — especially in the Richmond, Sunset, and dense Victorian-era neighborhoods — are too narrow or too built-out at the rear to expand horizontally. A 2nd story addition (sometimes called a "pop-top") adds square footage without touching your lot coverage, but it comes with real structural requirements: the existing foundation and framing have to be confirmed capable of carrying the new load, which frequently means a foundation retrofit as part of the project.

Good fit if:

  • Your rear yard is small, shared, or already built out
  • You want to keep your existing footprint and outdoor space intact
  • You're already planning structural or foundation work

Rear Addition: The Straightforward Expansion

A horizontal addition off the back of the house is generally the least structurally complex of the three — you're extending the existing first floor rather than adding load above it. It works best when there's usable rear yard depth and your setback requirements allow it.

Good fit if:

  • You have rear yard space you're not using heavily
  • You want a bigger kitchen, family room, or primary suite without touching the upper floor
  • You'd rather avoid the structural scope of building a new floor

ADU: When the Goal Is a Separate Unit, Not More Room in Your House

A 2nd story or rear addition adds space to your existing home. An ADU is a different animal entirely — a self-contained unit with its own kitchen, bathroom, and entrance, usable as a rental, an in-law unit, or space for adult children. If your real goal is income or a fully independent living space rather than more square footage in your own home, an ADU is the right category, not an addition.
