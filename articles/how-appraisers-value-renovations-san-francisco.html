How Appraisers Value a Renovated Home in San Francisco

By Solid Bay Construction SF | Updated 2026

Homeowners planning a renovation to build equity often assume the appraisal will simply reflect what they spent. It doesn't work that way. An appraisal is an estimate of market value based on comparable sales, not a receipt for construction costs. Understanding what an appraiser actually looks at is the difference between a renovation that builds real, bankable equity and one that just feels nicer to live in.

Comps Set the Ceiling

An appraiser's job is to support a value using recent comparable sales in your neighborhood — not to price your specific finish choices. If comparable homes on your block have sold in the $450/sq ft range, a renovation finished to a $650/sq ft standard will struggle to appraise at cost, no matter how well it's built. This is the single biggest reason high-end renovations in transitional or mixed-value blocks sometimes disappoint at appraisal time, even when the workmanship is excellent.

This cuts the other way too: in a neighborhood where comps are strong and buildable square footage is scarce, adding legal living space can appraise for meaningfully more than it cost to build — which is exactly the gap the equity-and-refinance strategy depends on.

Permitted vs. Unpermitted Space

An appraiser working from the county assessor's record and the permit history will exclude or heavily discount any square footage that isn't reflected in a final, signed-off DBI permit. A converted garage or finished basement done without permits might look identical to a permitted version from the inside — but on paper, to an appraiser and to any lender relying on that appraisal, it may as well not exist. What This Means for Renovation Planning

  • Prioritize square footage and room count over finish-level upgrades if equity growth is the primary goal
  • Match your finish level to your neighborhood's comps — a kitchen appropriate for the block outperforms an objectively nicer kitchen that's out of step with it
  • Always finalize permits before assuming any added space counts toward value
  • Talk to a local appraiser or real estate professional before finalizing scope if the renovation's ROI is a major factor in the decision

Frequently Asked Questions

Do appraisers care about high-end finishes like custom cabinetry or designer fixtures?

Somewhat, but far less than homeowners expect. Appraisers primarily value square footage, layout, and condition relative to comps — finish level matters at the margins, not dollar-for-dollar.

Will a renovation ever appraise for more than it cost to build?

Yes, especially for projects adding legal square footage or bed/bath count in a supply-constrained market like San Francisco. Purely cosmetic remodels are less likely to appraise above cost.
