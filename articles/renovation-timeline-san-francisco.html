San Francisco Renovation Timeline: What to Expect Month by Month

By Solid Bay Construction SF | Updated 2026

The most common question we get after cost is "how long will this actually take?" Here's a realistic month-by-month timeline for a mid-to-large SF renovation, from first call to final walkthrough.

By Project Type

ProjectTypical Duration
Bathroom renovation6–12 weeks
Kitchen remodel3–6 months
Garage conversion3–5 months
Full home remodel9–18 months
ADU (ground-up or conversion)12–24 months

Months 1–2: Scope, Design & Estimate

We walk the property, identify what's structural versus cosmetic, and develop a design and a written, itemized scope of work — not a verbal ballpark. For projects needing architectural or structural drawings, this phase includes that design work.

Months 2–5: Permitting

This is the phase most homeowners underestimate. A simple over-the-counter permit takes 1–5 business days, but most renovation-scale projects need DBI plan check (4–10 weeks) and sometimes SF Planning Department review as well (adding another 8–16 weeks on top). An in-house permit expeditor who works with SF DBI regularly can cut these timelines by 30–50% through pre-application meetings and fast turnaround on comment letters — but it's still the single biggest source of schedule uncertainty in any SF renovation.

Construction: Demo Through Finishes

Once permits are issued, construction proceeds against the approved plans, scheduled around required DBI inspections at each phase — foundation, framing, rough electrical/plumbing/HVAC, insulation, and final. Actual construction duration depends entirely on project scope: a bathroom is measured in weeks, a full remodel in many months.

Final Weeks: Inspections & Walkthrough

The last phase includes final DBI inspection and sign-off, punch-list items, and a final walkthrough before the project is considered complete. Disclosed milestones throughout the project — rather than surprises at the end — are what make this phase quick rather than another round of negotiation.

What Actually Causes Delays

  • Permit comment letters that sit unanswered for weeks
  • Hidden conditions discovered once walls are opened (common in 80–100+ year old SF housing stock)
  • Owner-initiated scope changes mid-construction
  • Material lead times on custom or special-order finishes
