San Francisco Foundation Repair: Costs & Warning Signs for Older Homes

By Solid Bay Construction SF | Updated 2026

Most of San Francisco's housing stock is 80–100+ years old, sitting on foundations that were never engineered to modern standards. Here's how to recognize a real foundation problem early, before it becomes a much larger repair.

Warning Signs to Watch For

  • Doors and windows that stick or no longer latch or close flush — a sign the frame around them has shifted
  • Diagonal cracks in interior drywall, especially radiating from door and window corners
  • Cracks in exterior stucco or siding, particularly stair-step cracks along mortar or panel lines
  • Uneven or sloping floors — noticeable when a ball placed on the floor rolls consistently in one direction
  • Gaps forming between walls and ceiling, or between walls and floor trim
  • A basement or crawl space with visible cracks in the foundation wall itself, or signs of water intrusion

Any one of these alone isn't necessarily urgent — but several appearing together, or worsening over a few months, is worth having assessed before it's ignored into a bigger repair.

What Causes Foundation Problems in SF Specifically

Much of San Francisco was built on a mix of bedrock, sand, and reclaimed land, with foundation quality varying block by block. Homes built before modern seismic codes often have shallow, undersized foundations that were adequate for their era but weren't designed for today's loads — especially once a home has had additions, a converted garage, or a new story added over the decades without corresponding foundation upgrades.

Foundation Repair vs. Seismic Retrofit

Foundation repair addresses existing damage or deterioration. Seismic retrofit — cripple wall bracing and foundation bolting — is a separate, proactive upgrade to help the structure perform better in an earthquake. Many projects combine both when foundation work is already underway, since it's far more cost-effective to do them together than as two separate projects years apart.
