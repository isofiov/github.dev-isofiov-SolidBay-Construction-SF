San Francisco is one of the most expensive, most regulated, and most competitive construction markets in the country. Between DBI permitting, historic preservation rules, seismic considerations, and a housing stock that's often 80–100+ years old, hiring the wrong contractor here can be a very expensive mistake.

What to Check Before You Sign

Verify the license — don't just take their word for it

Any contractor working on a project over $500 in California must hold an active Contractors State License Board (CSLB) license. Before hiring anyone, ask for their CSLB license number and look it up yourself at cslb.ca.gov to confirm it's active, in good standing, and covers the right classification. A contractor who's reluctant to share their license number is an immediate red flag.

Confirm insurance — general liability and workers' comp

San Francisco job sites carry real risk: scaffolding, demolition, structural work, older buildings with unknown conditions. Your contractor should carry general liability insurance to protect your property, and workers' compensation insurance to protect you from liability if a worker is injured on-site. Ask for a certificate of insurance directly from the carrier, not just a verbal assurance.

Understand how they handle permits

San Francisco's Department of Building Inspection (DBI) has its own quirks, review timelines, and inspection sequences. A contractor who regularly pulls permits in SF specifically — not just "the Bay Area" generally — will move your project through plan check, inspections, and sign-off far more predictably. Ask directly: "How many permits have you pulled with SF DBI in the last year?"

Ask about experience with SF-specific building conditions

San Francisco's housing stock creates recurring challenges a generalist contractor may not be prepared for: steep lots and hillside foundations, older homes with unpermitted prior work, seismic retrofit requirements, row houses with shared walls, and historic district design review. A contractor with real SF experience will ask you about these before you have to ask them.

Get a real, written scope of work

Vague estimates ("kitchen remodel — $60,000") lead to disputes. A trustworthy contractor provides an itemized scope covering materials, labor, timeline, and payment milestones tied to project progress — not large deposits collected upfront.

Look at recent, local work

Photos from ten years ago or projects in another state tell you very little. Ask for recent examples of work completed in San Francisco or Marin County, ideally in a similar style or scope to your project.

Read the contract terms carefully

For California home improvement projects over $50,000, state law (Business & Professions Code §7159) requires specific contract disclosures — a notice of your right to cancel, a payment schedule tied to completed work, and clear timelines. A contractor who provides this without being asked is signaling a compliant, professional operation.

Quick gut check: if a contractor won't give you a license number, won't provide a certificate of insurance, or wants a large upfront deposit before any written scope of work — walk away.

FAQ

How do I verify a contractor's license in California?

Search their license number directly at cslb.ca.gov. This shows license status, classification, bond information, and any disciplinary history.

What's the difference between a general contractor and a specialty contractor?

A general contractor (License Classification B) can oversee projects involving multiple trades — framing, electrical, plumbing — under one license. Specialty contractors are licensed for a single trade only.

Do I need a permit for a kitchen or bathroom remodel in San Francisco?

Most remodels involving plumbing, electrical, or structural changes require a DBI permit. A licensed contractor will handle this as part of the project.
