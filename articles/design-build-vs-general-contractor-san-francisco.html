Design-Build vs. General Contractor vs. Architect-First: Which Is Right for Your SF Renovation?

By Solid Bay Construction SF | Updated 2026

Before you hire anyone, there's a structural decision that shapes your entire project: do you hire an architect first and bid the design to contractors, hire a general contractor who brings in outside designers as needed, or work with a design-build firm that handles both under one roof? Here's an honest look at each.

Comparing the Three Models

Architect-First

You hire an architect independently, develop full design drawings, then bid the completed design to multiple contractors. This gives you competitive pricing on a finished design and full architectural control — but it also means the cost of your vision isn't confirmed until design is already complete, and if the bids come back over budget, you're paying for redesign.

Traditional General Contractor

You bring your own architect or designer, and the GC executes construction. This works well if you already have a strong design relationship, but coordination between the design team and construction team runs through you — if a detail doesn't translate cleanly from drawing to buildable reality, you're often the one relaying information between two separate companies.

Design-Build

Design and construction sit under one team from the first site visit, so cost is checked against the design continuously rather than discovered after drawings are finished. It also means one point of contact and one point of accountability from initial scope through final inspection — no handoff between a design firm and a separate construction firm, and no ambiguity about who's responsible if something doesn't go as planned.

This is the model we operate under at Solid Bay: an in-house team of architects, structural engineers, designers, and a dedicated permit expeditor, working directly with our construction crews on every project.

Which Should You Choose?

If you already have an architect you trust and just need construction, a traditional GC relationship can work fine. If you're doing a highly custom, architecturally ambitious project and want to competitively bid a finished design, architect-first has real merit. For most SF homeowners who want cost certainty, a single point of accountability, and one team managing the entire process, design-build removes the most common source of renovation headaches: the gap between what was designed and what it actually costs to build.
