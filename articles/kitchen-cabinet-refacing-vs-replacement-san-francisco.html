Kitchen Cabinet Refacing vs. Full Replacement in San Francisco

By Solid Bay Construction SF | Updated 2026

Cabinetry is typically the single largest line item in a kitchen remodel budget. Refacing — keeping the existing cabinet boxes and replacing only doors, drawer fronts, and hardware — can cut that cost substantially, but it only makes sense under the right conditions.

When Refacing Makes Sense

  • The existing cabinet boxes are structurally sound, square, and free of water damage or warping
  • You're happy with the current kitchen layout and don't need to relocate cabinets, appliances, or plumbing
  • The goal is primarily a cosmetic refresh — new door style, updated hardware, a different finish

When Full Replacement Is the Better Investment

  • You're changing the kitchen layout — moving the sink, range, or island location
  • Existing boxes show water damage, particularly common under sinks in older SF homes
  • You want different storage configurations (deeper drawers, pull-out organizers, different cabinet depths) that refacing can't provide
  • The existing boxes are builder-grade particleboard rather than solid wood or quality plywood construction

A Middle-Ground Option

Some SF kitchens are good candidates for a hybrid approach — refacing the majority of cabinets that are in good shape while fully replacing just the section being reconfigured (for example, adding a pantry cabinet or converting to a different island layout). This can capture much of the cost savings of refacing while still solving specific layout problems.

Don't Decide Before an In-Person Assessment

Photos and general condition descriptions aren't enough to know whether your specific cabinets are good refacing candidates — box material, square condition, and moisture damage all need to be checked in person before committing to either approach.
