How to Finance a Home Renovation in San Francisco

By Solid Bay Construction SF | Updated 2026

With SF renovation projects routinely running $200,000–$1,000,000+, very few homeowners pay cash outright. Here's how the main financing options actually work, and which situations each one fits best.

Home Equity Line of Credit (HELOC)

A HELOC lets you borrow against your home's existing equity as a revolving credit line, drawing funds as needed rather than all at once. This makes it a natural fit for construction, since you draw against it to make milestone payments as work is completed rather than committing to the full loan amount on day one.

Best for:

  • Projects with some scope flexibility or phased work
  • Homeowners who already have substantial equity and want to avoid refinancing a low existing mortgage rate

Cash-Out Refinance

A cash-out refinance replaces your existing mortgage with a new, larger one, and you receive the difference in cash to fund the renovation. It makes the most sense when current mortgage rates are at or below your existing rate, or when the project is large enough that a single lump sum is more practical than a draw-based line of credit.

Best for:

  • Large, well-defined projects with a fixed budget
  • Homeowners not currently locked into a much lower rate on their existing mortgage

Renovation-Specific Loans (FHA 203k, Fannie Mae HomeStyle)

These loans roll the cost of renovation into a single mortgage, based on the home's value after the work is complete rather than its current value — useful when buying a home that needs significant work, or refinancing to fund a major renovation without a separate second loan.

Best for:

  • Buying a fixer-upper and financing the renovation as part of the purchase
  • Homeowners without enough existing equity for a HELOC or cash-out refinance to cover the full project

What This Means for Your Contract

Whichever option you choose, a legitimate contractor's payment schedule should be tied to completed milestones — foundation, framing, rough-in, finishes — not large deposits collected upfront. That structure works cleanly with all three financing paths above, since you're drawing or disbursing funds as the corresponding work is actually done.
