Aging in Place Remodeling in San Francisco & Marin

By Solid Bay Construction SF | Updated 2026

Most homeowners who want to age in place aren't looking for an institutional, hospital-feeling renovation — they want a home that quietly works for them as mobility changes, without looking like it was built for that purpose. Here's what that actually involves in San Francisco and Marin's older housing stock.

Bathroom: The Highest-Priority Room

  • Zero-threshold (curbless) showers eliminate the trip hazard of a tub or curb step and work for both walking and wheelchair access
  • Blocking in the walls for grab bars, even if you don't install them yet — adding solid backing during a remodel costs almost nothing, but retrofitting it later means opening the wall again
  • Comfort-height toilets and widened doorways (32"+ clear width) for wheelchair or walker clearance
  • Slip-resistant flooring rated for wet areas, avoiding high-gloss tile

The San Francisco Row-House Challenge

Many SF Victorians and Edwardians put the only full bathroom upstairs, with bedrooms on the second or third floor and no ground-floor sleeping option. A genuine aging-in-place remodel often means converting a ground-floor room — a den, dining room, or rear addition — into a bedroom with an adjacent accessible bathroom, so daily living doesn't require the stairs.

Kitchen Adjustments

  • Pull-out shelving and drawers instead of deep base cabinets that require bending and reaching
  • A section of lower counter height (or a fixed seated work surface) for wheelchair users
  • Lever-style faucet handles and D-shaped cabinet pulls, easier to operate with limited grip strength
  • Front-control or side-opening appliances where possible

Whole-House Considerations

Lighting levels that were fine at 40 often aren't enough at 75 — adding layered, brighter lighting (especially at stairs and thresholds) is one of the highest-value, lowest-cost changes. If stairs are unavoidable, a straight-run stair with room for a future stair lift install is far cheaper to plan for now than to retrofit into a curved or narrow staircase later.
