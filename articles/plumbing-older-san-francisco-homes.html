Plumbing in Older San Francisco Homes: What to Expect

By Solid Bay Construction SF | Updated 2026

A large share of San Francisco's housing was plumbed before modern copper and PVC became standard. If your home hasn't had a full re-pipe, there's a good chance some of the original plumbing is still in service — and renovation is usually the most cost-effective time to address it.

What's Commonly Found in Pre-1960s SF Homes

  • Galvanized steel supply pipe — corrodes from the inside over decades, reducing water pressure and eventually leaking; most galvanized pipe installed before 1960 is well past its practical service life
  • Cast iron drain lines — durable but prone to cracking and root intrusion with age, especially at joints
  • Aging sewer laterals — the pipe connecting your home to the city sewer main is the homeowner's responsibility in San Francisco, and older clay or cast-iron laterals are a common source of backups
  • Undersized water service — older homes often have a 3/4" water service that struggles to support modern fixture counts and simultaneous use

Signs It's Time for an Upgrade

Discolored or rust-colored water, noticeably low pressure at multiple fixtures, recurring drain backups, or a plumber recommending "spot repairs" more than once in a few years are all signs that the underlying system — not just one fixture — needs attention.

Why Renovation Is the Right Time

Once walls are already open for a kitchen or bathroom remodel, the incremental cost of re-piping the rest of the home drops significantly compared to doing it as a standalone project later — you're not paying twice to open and re-close the same walls. Sewer lateral inspection is also required by SF's Sewer Lateral Ordinance at time of sale or major remodel in many cases, so it's worth addressing proactively rather than being forced into it during a transaction.
