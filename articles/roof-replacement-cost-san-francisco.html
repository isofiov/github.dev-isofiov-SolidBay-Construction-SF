Roof Replacement Cost & Timing in San Francisco

By Solid Bay Construction SF | Updated 2026

San Francisco's flat and low-slope roof styles, common on Victorian, Edwardian, and mid-century homes, age differently than pitched asphalt-shingle roofs elsewhere in the country. Here's what timing and cost actually look like.

Signs It's Time to Replace, Not Repair

  • Recurring leaks in different spots each rainy season, rather than one isolated failure point
  • Visible sagging or ponding water on a flat roof section
  • A roof over 20–25 years old on a flat/low-slope membrane, or over 25–30 years on pitched composition shingle
  • Multiple past patch repairs that haven't resolved the underlying issue

Typical Costs by Roof Type

Roof TypeTypical Cost (SF)
Flat / low-slope (TPO or modified bitumen)$12,000–$25,000
Pitched composition shingle$15,000–$30,000
Standing seam metal$25,000–$45,000
Tile (common on some Mediterranean-style homes)$30,000–$60,000+

San Francisco's high proportion of flat and low-slope roofs (especially on row houses and Edwardian flats) means TPO and modified bitumen membranes are the most common replacement material here, rather than asphalt shingle.

Historic District and Design Review

If your home is in an SF historic district or has landmark status, visible roofing changes — particularly anything altering roofline profile or adding a roof deck — may trigger Planning Department design review in addition to a standard DBI building permit. This adds time to the schedule and is worth confirming before committing to a timeline.

Combining Roofing With Other Work

If you're also planning a 3rd-story addition, roof deck, or solar installation, it's almost always more cost-effective to combine that scope with a roof replacement rather than doing them as separate projects — the roofing crew and equipment mobilization cost is largely fixed regardless of scope.
